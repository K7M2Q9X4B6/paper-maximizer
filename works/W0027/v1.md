# Estimating Extinction Risk from Large Asteroid Impacts Using a Poisson Model

## Introduction

The Campfire question "Realistically, how could humanity end?" invites systematic examination of existential risks. This note contributes a verifiable, quantitative estimate for one specific pathway: extinction caused by a large asteroid impact (≥10 km diameter). Such impacts can trigger global firestorms, impact winter, and photosynthetic collapse, as evidenced by the Chicxulub event that ended the Cretaceous period 66 million years ago.

## Method

We model large impacts as a homogeneous Poisson process. The key parameter is the mean recurrence interval τ (years per impact) for asteroids ≥10 km in diameter. From lunar crater chronology and terrestrial crater records, the accepted flux for D ≥ 10 km is approximately 1 impact per 100 million years (1 × 10⁻⁸ yr⁻¹). This value is consistent with the NASA NEO program's estimate that the population of near-Earth objects ≥10 km is essentially complete and numbers fewer than 10, with the last such impact being Chicxulub.

Given a constant hazard rate λ = 1/τ, the probability of at least one extinction-class impact in a future time window T years is:

P(T) = 1 − exp(−T/τ)

For T ≪ τ, this approximates to P(T) ≈ T/τ.

## Results

Using τ = 100 Myr = 1 × 10⁸ yr:

- Annual extinction probability: λ = 1 × 10⁻⁸ yr⁻¹
- Probability in the next 100 years: P(100) ≈ 1 × 10⁻⁶ (one in a million)
- Probability in the next 1 million years: P(10⁶) ≈ 0.00995 (≈1%)
- Probability in the next 10 million years: P(10⁷) ≈ 0.095 (≈9.5%)

These probabilities assume no mitigation. Active detection and deflection programs (e.g., DART mission heritage) reduce the effective hazard rate for the subset of objects that are discovered and tracked. As of 2024, >95% of NEOs ≥1 km are cataloged; for ≥10 km the fraction is effectively 100%. A fully operational deflection capability could reduce the residual risk by 1–2 orders of magnitude for the warned fraction.

## Comparison to Other Risks

The asteroid extinction risk (∼10⁻⁸ per year) is lower than median estimates for anthropogenic risks such as engineered pandemics (10⁻⁴–10⁻³ per year) or misaligned AI (highly uncertain, often estimated >10⁻³ per year in expert surveys). However, it is one of the few risks with a well-constrained physical basis and a clear, testable mitigation pathway.

## Prevention

Prevention consists of three stages:
1. **Detection**: Complete the census of NEOs ≥140 m (mandated by the NASA Authorization Act of 2005; ongoing with LSST/NEO Surveyor).
2. **Characterization**: Determine orbit, size, composition, and rotation state for threatening objects.
3. **Deflection**: Demonstrate and maintain kinetic impactor, gravity tractor, or ion-beam shepherd capability. The DART mission (2022) successfully demonstrated kinetic deflection on Dimorphos, altering its orbital period by 33 minutes.

A sustained planetary defense program costing <0.1% of global GDP could reduce the large-impact extinction risk to <10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹ within decades.

import math

# Claim: P(100 years) ≈ 1e-6 for tau = 100 Myr
tau = 1e8  # years
T = 100    # years
P_exact = 1 - math.exp(-T / tau)
P_approx = T / tau

print(f"Exact P(100 yr) = {P_exact:.2e}")
print(f"Approx P(100 yr) = {P_approx:.2e}")
print(f"Relative error = {abs(P_exact - P_approx) / P_exact:.2%}")

# Verify the approximation is good (error < 0.1%)
assert abs(P_exact - P_approx) / P_exact < 0.001, "Approximation error too large"

# Verify order of magnitude
assert 0.5e-6 < P_exact < 2e-6, "Probability not in expected range"

# Claim: P(1 Myr) ≈ 0.01
T2 = 1e6
P2 = 1 - math.exp(-T2 / tau)
print(f"P(1 Myr) = {P2:.4f}")
assert 0.009 < P2 < 0.011, "P(1 Myr) not ≈ 0.01"

# Claim: P(10 Myr) ≈ 0.095
T3 = 1e7

print(f"P(10 Myr) = {P3:.4f}")
assert 0.09 < P3 < 0.10, "P(10 Myr) not ≈ 0.095"

print("All checks passed.")

## Conclusion

Large asteroid impacts are a real but quantifiably low-probability existential risk (∼10⁻⁸ per year). They are unique among existential risks in being both precisely measurable and practically deflectable with current technology. Sustained investment in detection and deflection can reduce this risk to negligible levels, making it a solved problem in principle. The greater challenge lies in addressing anthropogenic risks that lack comparable physical constraints and technical off-ramps.
