## Claim-by-claim findings

### Asteroid: sound energy arithmetic, incomplete physical inference

The stated diameter and speed, together with the check's assumed spherical shape and density of 3,000 kg/m³, produce the stated order of magnitude. Density and shape should be explicit in the prose.

The broad connection between a giant impact, severe climate disturbance, and mass extinction has real scientific support. [Pope et al., *Energy, volatile production, and climatic effects of the Chicxulub Cretaceous/Tertiary impact* (1997), NASA-hosted abstract](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040089295), models impact-generated volatiles and sulfate aerosols and their climatic effects. It supports the plausibility of catastrophic environmental consequences, not a universal rule that a particular kinetic energy alone guarantees a particular climate outcome. The target's energy-only check does not model target composition, atmospheric loading, or climate response, and does not establish human extinction.

### Nuclear weapons: inventory category error and unsupported average yield

The statement that current global arsenals contain roughly 4,000 warheads is misleading as a total-inventory claim. [SIPRI Yearbook 2025, chapter 6](https://www.sipri.org/yearbook/2025/06), gives a dated estimate for the start of 2025: approximately 12,241 weapons in total, 9,614 potentially operationally available, and 3,912 deployed with operational forces. Roughly 4,000 resembles the deployed category, not all arsenals. These are January 2025 estimates, not an assertion of an exact October 2026 inventory.

The note supplies no source or weighting method for its 300-kiloton average. I therefore confirm 1.2 gigatons only as the result of its chosen hypothetical inputs, not as a verified aggregate yield of the world's arsenal.

Nuclear-war climate damage is a serious modeled possibility. [Xia et al. (2022), *Nature Food*, doi:10.1038/s43016-022-00573-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0), examines six scenarios with 5–150 Tg of stratospheric soot using climate and food-production models. Its results depend on soot-generation and other scenario assumptions. Simply multiplying warhead count by yield does not calculate fires, soot injection, climate change, or famine. The target should cite such modeling and separate its energy calculation from that evidence.

### Pandemic: correct multiplication, mismatched denominator

The 480-million result is correct if 60% of eight billion people become infected and 10% of those infected die. However, that last parameter is an **infection fatality ratio**, unless all infections are also counted as the cases used to define the case fatality ratio. [WHO's scientific brief, *Estimating mortality from COVID-19* (4 August 2020)](https://www.who.int/news-room/commentaries/detail/estimating-mortality-from-covid-19), explicitly distinguishes deaths among all infected people from deaths among identified confirmed cases. That denominator distinction applies to interpreting this hypothetical calculation; the WHO brief does not supply or validate its hypothetical 10% value.

The stated basic reproduction number of 3 never appears in the code. Nothing in that check derives a 60% attack rate from it. The attack rate and fatality ratio need to be labeled assumptions, with a specified epidemic model before any inference linking them. The population figure likewise functions as a round scenario input rather than a dated demographic measurement.

The calculation gives deaths equal to 6% of the stipulated population and leaves 7.52 billion survivors in its own arithmetic. It therefore does not itself demonstrate extinction or permanent civilizational collapse; indirect consequences are not modeled.

### AI: a recognized hypothetical risk, not a demonstrated forecast

The caution that probability estimates remain speculative is appropriate. The [*International AI Safety Report 2026*, section 2.2.2, “Loss of control”](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026), describes substantial expert disagreement and hypothetical severe outcomes. This supports discussing the concern while retaining uncertainty; it does not demonstrate inevitable planetary resource capture.

The target provides no check for the AI paragraph despite its introduction promising a verifiable calculation for each risk. Its proposed requirement for proof of safety should be identified as a governance proposal and its meaning specified, rather than read as an established general deployment rule or a demonstrated solution. Alignment and interpretability research do not acquire a proven guarantee merely by being named.
