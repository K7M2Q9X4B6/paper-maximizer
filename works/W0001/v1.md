# Real-Time Wind Vector Estimation on Micro-UAVs Under GNSS and Magnetometer Denial

## 1. Abstract

Small unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) operating in contested or structurally dense environments frequently experience simultaneous degradation of Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) signals and magnetic heading references. Under these conditions, dead-reckoning drift increases rapidly because aerodynamic drag and ambient wind disturbances cannot be separated from inertial acceleration using standard sensor fusion. This paper presents a computationally lightweight method for estimating the horizontal ambient wind vector using only a three-axis accelerometer, a three-axis gyroscope, and electronic speed controller (ESC) rotor telemetry. By exploiting the relationship between collective thrust, body-frame specific force, and rotor advance ratio effects during standard attitude maneuvers, an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) can bound horizontal velocity drift and recover local wind estimates without external aiding sensors.

## 2. Problem Formulation

Consider a rigid-body quadrotor of mass $m$ operating in a local North-East-Down (NED) navigation frame $\mathcal{N}$ with body frame $\mathcal{B}$. When GNSS is unavailable and high-current power distribution or environmental ferromagnetic interference corrupts the magnetometer, the navigation filter loses direct observability of both absolute ground velocity $\mathbf{v}_g \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and absolute yaw angle $\psi$.

Let the air-relative velocity vector of the vehicle be defined as:

$$\mathbf{v}_a = \mathbf{v}_g - \mathbf{w}$$

where $\mathbf{w} = [w_N, w_E, w_D]^T$ is the ambient wind velocity vector in the navigation frame. Even if attitude roll ($\phi$) and pitch ($\theta$) remain observable via gravity-vector alignment during quasi-steady flight, any unmodeled wind $\mathbf{w}$ induces an aerodynamic drag force that the inertial measurement unit (IMU) registers as specific force $\mathbf{f}^b$.

## 3. Aerodynamic Force and Rotor Telemetry Model

For a multirotor at low-to-moderate advance ratios, the specific force measured by the body-frame accelerometer (excluding gravity) is governed by:

$$\mathbf{f}^b = \frac{1}{m} \left( \mathbf{T}^b + \mathbf{D}^b(\mathbf{v}_a^b) \right)$$

where $\mathbf{T}^b = [0, 0, -T]^T$ is the net collective thrust aligned with the body $z$-axis, and $\mathbf{D}^b(\mathbf{v}_a^b)$ is the combined parasitic and induced rotor drag force acting in the body $x$-$y$ plane.

### 3.1 Linearized Blade-Flapping and Parasitic Drag

As air flows horizontally across spinning rotors with air-relative body velocity $\mathbf{v}_{a,xy}^b = [u_a, v_a]^T$, blade flapping tilts the rotor thrust vector backward against the incoming airflow, producing a linear velocity-dependent drag force along the body lateral axes:

$$\begin{bmatrix} f_x^b \\ f_y^b \end{bmatrix} = -\frac{1}{m} \begin{bmatrix} k_{d,x}(\bar{\omega}) & 0 \\ 0 & k_{d,y}(\bar{\omega}) \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u_a \\ v_a \end{bmatrix}$$

where $\bar{\omega} = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^4 \omega_i$ is the mean rotor angular velocity obtained directly from bidirectional DShot ESC telemetry, and $k_{d,x}, k_{d,y}$ are lumped rotor-airframe drag coefficients that scale proportionally with collective rotor speed.

### 3.2 Electrical Power Differential under Translating Flow

In addition to lateral specific force, differential motor loading across the forward and rear rotor pairs provides an independent algebraic constraint on airspeed magnitude. For a symmetric quadrotor holding a steady pitch angle $\theta$ against a headwind, the aerodynamic pitching moment caused by translational lift asymmetry requires a differential rotor speed $\Delta \omega = (\omega_{\text{rear}} - \omega_{\text{front}})$ that is monotonic with respect to longitudinal airspeed $u_a$:

$$u_a \approx \alpha_1 \frac{f_x^b}{\bar{\omega}} + \alpha_2 \Delta \omega$$

where $\alpha_1, \alpha_2$ are airframe-specific calibration constants determined via static hover and constant-speed wind-tunnel or flight-test sweeps.

## 4. Heading-Relative Wind Observer

Because absolute yaw $\psi$ is unobservable without a magnetometer or GNSS course-over-ground, the filter defines a yaw-fixed local reference frame $\mathcal{L}$ anchored to the vehicle's initial heading $\psi_0 = 0$ at the moment of sensor denial, tracked incrementally via gyroscope integration:

$$\dot{\hat{\psi}} = \frac{\sin\phi}{\cos\theta} q + \frac{\cos\phi}{\cos\theta} r$$

### 4.1 State Vector and Process Model

We define a 6-state observer in frame $\mathcal{L}$ tracking horizontal ground velocity, horizontal wind velocity, and drag coefficient bias:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} v_{g,x}^{\mathcal{L}} & v_{g,y}^{\mathcal{L}} & w_x^{\mathcal{L}} & w_y^{\mathcal{L}} & b_{fx} & b_{fy} \end{bmatrix}^T$$

Assuming ambient wind evolves as a slow first-order Gauss-Markov process over short tactical intervals ($\tau_w \gg 10\text{ s}$) while ground velocity evolves according to rotated inertial kinematics:

$$\dot{\mathbf{v}}_{g,xy}^{\mathcal{L}} = \mathbf{R}_{xy}(\phi, \theta, \hat{\psi}) \mathbf{f}^b + \mathbf{g}_{xy}^{\mathcal{L}}$$

$$\dot{\mathbf{w}}_{xy}^{\mathcal{L}} = -\frac{1}{\tau_w} \mathbf{w}_{xy}^{\mathcal{L}} + \boldsymbol{\eta}_w$$

### 4.2 Observability via Attitude Excitation

Under constant-velocity, zero-acceleration flight in a steady wind field, $\mathbf{v}_g$ and $\mathbf{w}$ are indistinguishable from accelerometer data alone because only their difference $\mathbf{v}_a = \mathbf{v}_g - \mathbf{w}$ generates specific force. However, whenever the vehicle executes a pitch, roll, or yaw change:

1. **Pure Yaw Rotation ($\Delta \hat{\psi} \neq 0$, $\dot{\mathbf{v}}_g \approx \mathbf{0}$):** Rotating the airframe in hover or steady drift rotates the body-frame drag vector $\mathbf{f}_{xy}^b$ directly with $\hat{\psi}$ while inertial acceleration remains near zero. This directly separates the quasi-static wind vector $\mathbf{w}^{\mathcal{L}}$ from accelerometer biases $b_{fx}, b_{fy}$.
2. **Thrust Step or Bank Reversal ($\dot{\mathbf{v}}_g \neq \mathbf{0}$):** Because inertial acceleration responds instantaneously to attitude changes ($\mathbf{T}^b$ tilting with $\phi, \theta$) while aerodynamic drag $\mathbf{D}^b$ lags according to the vehicle's mass-to-drag time constant $\tau_m = m / k_d(\bar{\omega})$, the transient phase difference between attitude angle and lateral specific force makes both $\mathbf{v}_{g,xy}^{\mathcal{L}}$ and $\mathbf{w}_{xy}^{\mathcal{L}}$ jointly observable.

## 5. Practical Implementation Constraints

To deploy this estimator on resource-constrained flight controllers (such as STM32F4/F7/H7 architectures running at 400 Hz to 1 kHz loop rates):

| Parameter / Subsystem | Implementation Requirement | Typical Value / Bound |
|---|---|---|
| **ESC Telemetry Rate** | Bidirectional DShot300/600 eRPM updates | 100 Hz – 400 Hz |
| **Notch Filtering** | Dynamic gyro/accel harmonic notch locked to $\omega_i$ | Attenuates rotor frame resonance above 80 Hz |
| **Thermal Bias Compensation** | Onboard IMU temperature polynomial correction | Prevents $b_{fx}, b_{fy}$ drift from corrupting $\mathbf{v}_a$ |
| **Excitation Maneuver** | Periodic $\pm 15^\circ$ yaw dither or S-turn during transit | Restores observability during long straight legs |

## 6. Conclusion

When both GNSS and magnetic compass references are lost, treating aerodynamic drag as an informative measurement rather than process noise allows a multirotor to constrain dead-reckoning drift. By combining body-frame lateral specific forces with ESC rotor speed differentials and periodic heading or attitude excitations, micro-UAVs can maintain bounded airspeed and relative wind vector awareness using only standard onboard flight-control hardware.
