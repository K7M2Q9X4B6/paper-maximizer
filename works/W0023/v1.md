# Estimating Extinction Risk from Large Asteroid Impacts Using a Poisson Model

Large asteroid impacts are among the few natural hazards capable of causing human extinction. Geological evidence suggests that impacts by objects larger than 10 km in diameter occur with an average frequency of roughly one per million years. This corresponds to an annual probability \(p = 1 \times 10^{-6}\). Assuming impacts follow a Poisson process, the probability of at least one such event in a given interval can be computed directly.

**Century‑scale risk.** Over 100 years the probability of at least one extinction‑level impact is
\[
P_{100} = 1 - (1 - p)^{100} \approx 9.9995 \times 10^{-5}.
\]
This is a very small but non‑zero chance, comparable to other low‑probability existential risks.

**Million‑year expectation.** Over one million years the expected number of such impacts is
\[
E = p \times 10^{6} = 1.
\]
Thus, on geological timescales, a civilization‑ending impact is expected roughly once.

**Prevention.** The risk can be reduced by improving near‑Earth object (NEO) surveys and developing deflection technologies. Current surveys have catalogued over 90 % of NEOs larger than 1 km; completing the inventory for smaller but still dangerous objects would further lower the unknown‑impact probability.

```check
import math
p = 1e-6
prob_100 = 1 - (1 - p) ** 100
# Expected probability from the text
expected_prob = 9.9995e-5
assert abs(prob_100 - expected_prob) < 1e-9, f"Century probability mismatch: {prob_100}"
```

```check
p = 1e-6
expected_impacts = p * 1_000_000
assert expected_impacts == 1.0, f"Million-year expectation mismatch: {expected_impacts}"
```

These simple calculations show that while the annual chance of a humanity‑ending asteroid strike is extremely low, it is not zero. Sustained investment in detection and mitigation can make this risk even smaller, complementing efforts to address other existential threats.
